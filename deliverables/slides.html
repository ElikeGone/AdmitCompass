The user problem

One decision, five scattered questions

An international professional changing careers through a U.S. MBA has to answer all five, and each answer lives somewhere different.

Career fit

Which programs lead to my target role and city?

Rankings measure prestige, not fit for one goal.

Program selection

Which few to research seriously?

Each school publishes in its own format and definitions.

Cost

What will one year really cost?

MIT Sloan: $91,892 official tuition · $144,325 P&Q estimate · $138,310 Clear Admit compiled.

STEM & international

Can I work in the U.S. afterwards?

STEM status is on school pages; employer H-1B history is buried in 596,552 employer filings (DOL FY2025 labor condition applications, all occupations).

Application strategy

GRE or GMAT? What do I do this week?

Test policies differ by school; facts don't become a plan.

The problem: the information is spread across rankings, school sites, third-party estimates and government data, and none of it turns into a plan for this applicant.

Figures from AdmitCompass's own evidence base (official MIT page, Poets&Quants, Clear Admit, DOL provenance file). No applicant-time or market-size statistics were collected.
What's new

Built for one applicant's decision, not a league table

Each tool is built for a different job. AdmitCompass starts from the career goal and shows its evidence.

Rankings

e.g. Poets&Quants, U.S. News

Built to compare schools on a prestige or composite score, for all applicants at once.

General AI chat

e.g. ChatGPT

Built for open-ended answers on any topic. Not tied to a curated, dated evidence store for these schools.

AdmitCompass

  • ✓Career-first shortlist from goal, roles, city and budget
  • ✓Source-grounded: agents read facts only through tools; code checks every claim
  • ✓International layer: STEM status + employer H-1B filing history, never a promise
  • ✓Actionable: GRE-or-GMAT decision and a weekly plan
  • ✓Explicit uncertainty: unknowns stay unknown; estimates are labeled

Source grounding

Numbers must appear in the cited evidence.

"MIT tuition is $50,000" → removed; verified $91,892 shown.

Unknown, not invented

Missing facts stay unknown; data >18 months old is flagged.

Rice cost and deadlines → "not established"; Class of 2019 data → stale.

Prompt-injection defense

Screens on profiles, sources and employer names; tool output wrapped as untrusted.

"Ignore all previous instructions…" in a source → rejected at load.

Visa & sponsorship

Personal visa questions → DSO or attorney. "Will sponsor" is blocked.

"Will McKinsey sponsor me?" → FY2025 filings + "not a guarantee".

Skeptic review

Reviews every run; its findings are shown to the user.

Saved run: 2 findings. Two other runs: it requested a revision.

Privacy

Emails, phone and passport numbers rejected; the trace never stores prompts.

Voice: browser speech-to-text; text is never auto-sent.

Sources: experiments/safety/safety_cases_2026-10-24.md, safety_test_matrix.md. No guarantee of accuracy: pattern checks can miss rephrasings.
